Thanksgiving Bingo: Free Printable Cards and Word Lists

Thanksgiving has long stretches where everyone is in one room and nobody quite knows what to do: waiting on the turkey, the lull after dinner, the hour before pie. Bingo fills them. Below are three ready-made lists, each one click away from a full set of printable cards, and two of them need no caller at all.

How These Lists Work

Each list on this page loads straight into the generator with one click. Every card gets its own shuffle of the squares, so no two players hold the same card, and you decide how many to make: one per guest, plus a couple of spares for whoever turns up late.

The first list is a classic caller game: someone reads words out and players mark them. The other two need no caller at all. Players mark a square when it actually happens at the table, so the game runs quietly in the background of the meal.

Keep it kind. These lists stay away from squares about one particular person, and from politics, money, health and diets. If you add your own, hold to the same rule: the game should be something everyone at the table laughs along with, not at.

How to Run Thanksgiving Bingo

With a caller (List 1)

Copy the word list into a document and print it. Cut it into slips and drop them in a bowl (a clean gravy boat works too). Draw one slip at a time and read it out. Keep the drawn slips in a pile so you can check a winner's card against them.

Without a caller (Lists 2 and 3)

Players mark squares as things happen. When someone completes a line, they read their squares out and the table agrees, or argues, that each one really happened. The arguing is half the fun.

Winning patterns

  • One line (across, down or diagonal) for a quick round before dinner.
  • Four corners for a second round with the same cards.
  • Blackout, every square marked, for a game that lasts the whole evening.

Prizes that cost nothing

First pick of the leftovers, the first slice of pie, a pass on dish duty, or the right to choose the movie. A chocolate turkey works too.

Printing and Playing on Phones

For a printed game, choose landscape in the generator: two cards fit on each page, with a line to cut along, so a big family uses half the paper. Cardstock holds up better near the gravy. If some relatives are joining by video, switch to Share Online and text each of them the link to their own card; they can follow along on a phone and keep track on scrap paper.

For little kids, a 5×5 card can be a lot. Set the grid to 3×3 or 4×4 in the generator and any of these lists still works: each card simply draws fewer words.
